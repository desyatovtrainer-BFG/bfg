LLM Council Verdict

BFG — Presence Relationship Progression System (communication cadence) · Product / Companion / Relationship design only
The question. Should the Presence's communication frequency evolve with the relationship — high early (Stages 1–3) → reduced (4–6) → rare (7–10) → very rare (Legend Era)? The Companion Doctrine already accepts the taper ("frequency tapers; temperature never does"). The genuinely new claim under test is binding the taper to Evolution Stage / Level as the control variable, in discrete bands.

Verdict: B — Promising, but requires changes

Taper principle: KEEP (already doctrine) Stage/Level as the knob: REJECT

The instinct is right and already canon. The mechanism is wrong: tying social cadence to a performance ladder turns progression into a thermostat — "the Presence loves me less for climbing" — recreating the forbidden ledger and silencing the very milestone (evolution) that must speak. Replace the control variable with relationship tenure + return/activity history + event-depletion, applied as a smooth curve, temperature never dropping. Not for MVP.

4 of 5 reject the stage-bound design. The lone defender argues for the taper principle — which the doctrine already grants. So the real council position: taper yes, by stage no.

Winner: C, but controlled by relationship tenure + event-depletion — not Evolution Stage. This is essentially what the Doctrine already prescribes (§XI taper + §V silence-first + §X no-ledger).

Chairman's five answers

1. Should frequency evolve? Yes — and it already does, by doctrine. The taper is correct.
2. What controls it? Relationship age (wall-clock tenure) + activity/return history (absence, re-engagement) + event-depletion (firsts run out). Not Level/Stage. Evolution Stage stays a visual/narrative reward.
3. Is decreasing comms desirable? Yes in principle — only if temperature never drops, it never reads as withdrawal-for-achievement, milestone moments always speak, the curve is smooth not cliffed, and returns re-open warmth.
4. Biggest mistake? Making cadence a function of the performance ladder — a social thermostat that recreates the forbidden ledger and pairs evolution (a guaranteed-celebration moment, Decision 035) with sudden silence.
5. Approve for MVP? No. Keep the current state-based cadence. Defer cadence-shaping; it's untestable pre-launch (months-long, churn-confounded loop). If tapering at all, base it on tenure/session count, smooth, instrumented.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian — stage is a social thermostat
Stage/level converts a performance ladder into a social thermostat — the exact ledger the doctrine forbids. The user feels its shadow: "the more I progress, the less it talks to me" = withdrawal-for-achievement, a warmth taper smuggled in through frequency. Stage isn't time: a high-activity user blasts Stages 1–3 in a week, hitting "rare" cadence while still a stranger; the slow beginner is trapped in high-frequency mode where the PRESSURE risk bites hardest. Discrete bands create a perceptible cliff at 3→4, pairing the evolution celebration with sudden quiet — silence becomes interpretable as feedback, which §VI bans. True variable: tenure + demonstrated security, not the XP rung.
The First Principles Thinker — wrong control variable, two clocks
The hypothesis proposes the wrong control variable; the doctrine already tapers safely and binding to stage removes that safety. It conflates two clocks — relationship maturity vs progression rank — which correlate early and diverge constantly (a returning lapsed user is high-stage but low-trust, so the system goes quiet on whoever needs re-bootstrapping). Frequency is an output, not a control. The real variable is EVENT STRUCTURE: which moments are rare and earned enough to justify breaking silence. Design moment-eligibility and the taper falls out for free — early "firsts" make speech dense; later, firsts are exhausted, so silence dominates. Reframe: not "how often per stage?" but "what makes a moment deserve a voice?"
The Expansionist — earning silence is the signature (defense)
Earning silence is BFG's underpriced asset. Constant contact is what competitors do and is worse — it makes a secure base needy. The taper is the felt signature of secure love (the parent who stops hovering; the old friend's comfortable silence). Scarcity converts each late word into an event; a Stage 9 sentence carries two years of witnessing. Bind the taper to stage and silence becomes narrative — "it speaks less because we've arrived," graduation not abandonment. No competitor can copy a two-year arc. It unlocks ritual, landmark gravity, anticipation as retention. The 10x version: silence becomes the product's deepest reward.
The Outsider — "it sounds like the app gave up on me"
I'd never read it as "we're close now" — I'd read it as the app giving up on me. Early on I'm not attached; the little creature talking is the hook. Right when I'm finally sticking with it and working hard for months, it goes quiet? Backwards — I worked harder and got less. Fewer messages reads as losing interest, not intimacy (a friend who texts less makes you think "did I do something?"). I probably wouldn't even notice the clever taper — I'd just feel it get quieter and emptier and assume I hit the end of the content, that the app's dead. Evolution with silence = cheated, not honored. "Old friends need fewer words" is a designer's poem; as the user, silence just feels like silence.
The Executor — don't ship stage-banded for MVP
Buildability is trivial (a lookup table); the design is the risk. Fatal flaw: stage proxies activity, not relationship time — a 5x/week user hits Stage 3 in ~3 weeks, a 1x/week beginner in 4+ months, so cadence goes quiet fastest for the most-engaged, backwards from retention. The taper inverts content cost vs need. A step function reads as rejection — ramp continuously. It's untestable: a months-long feedback loop confounded by churn. Stage is unstable (returns after absence break it). Verdict: do not ship stage-banded for MVP. Keep state-based phrases; add a calendar-tenure or session-count taper if any; make evolution stage a visual reward, not the cadence knob. Ship the bond, instrument it, learn.

Blind spots the peer review caught

What everyone missed

• No one challenged whether a paying subscriber should be tapered toward silence at all. Designed quiet in a subscription product raises "why am I paying for less?" — the Outsider intuited it; nobody priced it.

• No one proposed how to settle the disagreement empirically. "Felt graduation" (Expansionist) vs "felt abandonment" (Outsider) is testable via cohort retention + sentiment and a reversible rollout — yet the debate stayed at the level of the poem.

• No one raised user-controllable cadence as an alternative to any fixed curve (adaptive to lapse, individual sensitivity).

• Chairman addition: the stage-banded taper directly contradicts Decision 035 — every Evolution Stage must feel rewarding and memorable. Dropping a cadence band at the stage boundary would have the Presence go quieter exactly as the body transforms. That's a registry-level conflict, not just a vibe risk.
